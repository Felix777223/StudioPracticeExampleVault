Today I came across a project, credited in the post to zongze2222, in which turning a physical knob controls animations of cutting and peeling food. I found the relationship between the physical action and the visual response particularly interesting.

In my previous work, I often approached interaction as pressing a button to trigger a response. This project expanded my thinking because turning the knob creates an intuitive association with cutting, scraping and peeling, even though the hand movement does not directly imitate these actions.

The continuous feedback also caught my attention. As the knob turns, the animation keeps responding and the food is gradually processed. From watching the demonstration, this rhythm makes the interaction seem fluid and satisfying. The act of operating the device becomes part of the experience itself.

This made me want to explore how gestures and everyday associations could shape my own work. Rather than choosing an input device first, I could begin with an action or feeling I want the audience to experience, then consider how movement and feedback might communicate it.

As a next step, I would like to test a simple continuous interaction and explore how changes in its rhythm and visual response affect the experience.
